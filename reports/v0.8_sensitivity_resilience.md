# BiogasFlex v0.8 — Uncertainty and hypothetical critical-service security

**Release:** v0.8.0 local research-development candidate (2026-11-05); 117 automated tests pass. This report is a **conditional, counterfactual numerical study** — not a claim about observed Swedish biogas plant operations, enacted energy policy or a demonstrated island-capable CHP installation.

## Research question and design

How robust is the conditional market-timing value of storage-buffered CHP to the electricity-price window, heat-service demand and cost, gas inventory capacity, conversion efficiency, and power headroom? Separately, can a **hypothetical** precharged gas buffer serve a defined local critical electricity/heat load under a gas-supply interruption **if** plant islanding and CHP availability are explicitly assumed?

The data and scenario registry were selected before v0.8 result runs. Three complete historical day-ahead price windows (first local day of January, April and October 2020, SE3) are retained. July 1 was preselected but **rejected for a provider time-series gap**; its market result is not estimated or replaced. These three observed days are **not an annual price sample**. The model uses Lövsta's 2022 annual figures as a **scale reference** and hypothetical constant CHP/heat assumptions, so no actual plant hourly schedule or realized merchant revenue has been recreated.

## Economic study: 216 solved configurations

Six degrees of variation generate 3 observed price days × 3 buffer capacities × 2 heat-load factors × 2 backup cost coefficients × 3 assumed electric efficiencies × 2 electric rating cases = **216 predeclared solved scenarios**. The complete matrix, cases, input hashes and all null/positive cases are retained in `results/v0.8/`.

Overall conditional minimum is €0.00, raw numerical median ~€0.00, and maximum €15.7953 in a selected-day comparison. These are **finite-grid conditional extrema, not statistics about an annual market distribution**. The optimization can always imitate the fixed schedule, so its incremental contribution is **nonnegative by mathematical construction** whenever that fixed comparator is feasible. It therefore does *not* imply a gas vessel has nonnegative NPV or positive benefit after unmodeled CAPEX, startup, maintenance, operational constraints or site-specific heat costs.

**Demonstrated assumption reversal:** At the **same 2020-04-01 market window**, 1 MWh_LHV storage, heat-demand factor 1.0, electrical efficiency 0.42, and the recorded 499 kW CHP rating, assumed backup heat at €20/MWh_th produces **€0.5206** incremental scenario contribution while €80/MWh_th yields **€0.00** within numerical tolerance. That is a change in a **hypothetical scenario coefficient** — no observed Swedish heat supply tariff is inferred.

The result remains sensitive to **price spread, backup-heat substitution cost and generating headroom**, but 2020 single-day samples cannot establish seasonal or year-to-year robustness. Perfect price foresight and lack of start/ramp/part-load constraints make this a mathematical opportunity comparison, not executable plant scheduling or financial appraisal.

## Separate service-security stress case: five conditional states

A 3-hour hypothetical local gas-inflow outage with no grid or auxiliary heat input is defined. Critical demand is 0.12 MW_e and 0.10 MW_th each hour. Available CHP conversion efficiency is assumed as η_e=0.4, η_th≈0.391594; electrical rating 499 kW. Distinctly from normal economic dispatch, the emergency initial gas stock is exogenously **precharged**, and no cyclic/end inventory requirement applies. The LP prioritizes reducing electricity and heat energy deficits with synthetic 10:1 electric-to-heat penalty weights. It does **not** demonstrate black start, protection, switchgear, grid-forming operation, synchronization or safe islanding at Lövsta.

| Scenario | Precharged gas (MWh_LHV) | Islandable/available CHP? | Critical electricity served (MWh) | Critical heat served (MWh_th) |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| No buffer | 0.0 | Assumed yes | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| Buffer 0.3 | 0.3 | Assumed yes | 0.120 | 0.1175 |
| Buffer 0.6 | 0.6 | Assumed yes | 0.240 | 0.2350 |
| Buffer 0.6, not islandable | 0.6 | **No** | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| Buffer 0.6, CHP unavailable | 0.6 | **No** | 0.000 | 0.000 |

Total three-hour demanded energy is 0.36 MWh electric and 0.30 MWh thermal. With 0.6 MWh_LHV precharged gas and the capability assumptions enabled, roughly **66.7% of critical electricity** and **78.3% of critical heat** can be served on an *energy-integrated basis*. This does **not** establish three continuous hours of electric or heat supply; the LP does not include minimum stable load, starts, ramping, thermal inertia, gas pressure, physical islanded controls, time-dependent failure probabilities or gas replenishment during restoration. If the plant cannot island or the CHP is unavailable, the model correctly allows no CHP service despite stored gas.

**No regional resilience claim:** These hypothetical local critical-load service fractions are not regional electricity self-sufficiency, reliability statistics, policy value, outage probability, or a property of the real Lövsta facility. Comparing precharged-gas cases with zero-buffer cases changes available initial fuel; unlike the economic comparison, those cases are not equal-inventory dispatch comparisons.

## Independent-output numerical review

- **117 automated tests** retain all prior 81 v0.7 and earlier tests and add 36 v0.8 evidence/physical/tampering tests.
- `scripts/independent_v08_audit.py` uses only standard-library modules and rechecks **216 interval-level economic schedules** and **five emergency schedules** without importing the optimizer. It verifies observed price timestamps/values against the attributed snapshots, registry and source hashes, gas and heat conversion, heat-service balance, electricity value, backup heat, costs and solver status.
- Independent analytical emergency bounds are `0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12 MWh_e` and `0.6 × 0.4 = 0.24 MWh_e`, without grid import; corresponding heat outputs are calculated from the assumed thermal efficiency. Numerical equality of these bounded cases verifies a synthetic optimization instance, not a physical plant.
- Tampering tests deliberately corrupt gas stock, heat service, economic values, and solver metadata; the independent checker rejects them.
- This work is **self/AI-assisted code verification**. No independent human reviewer, plant-operator approval or empirical comparison is claimed.
